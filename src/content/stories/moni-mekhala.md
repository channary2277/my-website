Long, long ago, a great teacher lived in the sky. He had many students. Two of them were Moni Mekhala, the goddess of the sea, and Ream Eyso, a strong giant.

One day the teacher gave his students a test. "Bring me a cup of morning dew," he said. "The first one to bring it will win a prize."

Ream Eyso walked through the grass, collecting dew drop by drop. It was very slow.

Moni Mekhala was clever. At night, she put a cloth on the grass. In the morning, the cloth was wet with dew. She squeezed it into a cup, and she was first.

The teacher gave her the prize: a shining, magic crystal ball.

Ream Eyso was very angry. He wanted the crystal ball for himself. He took his big axe and chased Moni Mekhala across the sky.

But Moni Mekhala was not afraid. She held up her crystal ball, and it flashed with bright light. Ream Eyso could not see! Angry, he threw his axe. It crashed across the sky with a loud BOOM.

People in Cambodia say this is why, in the rainy season, we see lightning and hear thunder. It is Moni Mekhala's crystal ball flashing, and Ream Eyso's axe crashing, as they chase each other across the sky.
